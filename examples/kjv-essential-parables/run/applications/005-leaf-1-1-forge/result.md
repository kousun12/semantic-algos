# 005 · leaf-1-1-forge

- Function: repository `question-forge`
- Inputs: [leaf 1.1 surface question](../004-leaf-1-1-heart/result.md)
- Configuration: independent one-pass forge

## Result

## The question you asked

What does it mean for human hope and responsibility that God preserves humanity even as human sin repeatedly brings judgment, alienation, and division?

## What it's doing

The question honors a real tension between preservation and judgment, but it keeps that tension at the level of humanity in general and asks for its meaning from a safe interpretive distance. In doing so, it can protect the asker from the harder issue nearby: whether hope is merely a belief about what God will preserve, or a claim that makes demands on how one responds to the alienation and division one inherits, participates in, or can resist.

## The forged question

**If God's preservation means neither that human sin is harmless nor that judgment and division are the last word, what responsibility for the alienation I inherit, participate in, or can resist becomes mine—and what would it cost me to make my hope visible in how I bear that responsibility?**

## What changed

The question shifts its subject from humanity to the asker, moves from abstract meaning to lived obligation, and dramatizes hope as something that must become visible under conditions of judgment and division. It retains both sides of the original tension while removing the safety of a general theological answer: preservation cannot simply excuse responsibility, and judgment cannot simply cancel hope. The added question of cost gives the reformulation torque because any honest response may require relinquishing innocence, passivity, or a merely verbal hope.

## Living with it

To carry this question is to let ordinary encounters with blame, estrangement, inherited conflict, and possible repair test what hope is doing in practice. It need not be settled quickly; its work is to keep preservation, responsibility, and cost in the same frame whenever withdrawal or presumption would make one of them disappear.
