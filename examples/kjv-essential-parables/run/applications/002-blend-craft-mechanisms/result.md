## Result

### Anonymous craft brief

1. Build the narrative around a concrete human relation placed under pressure by a shared action, judgment, or institutional condition.
2. Maintain a witnessing stance that remains morally intimate and self-implicating rather than retreating into detached accusation.
3. Make abstract pressures legible through clear conceptual distinctions and their visible consequences for people acting together.
4. Use plurality as a structural force: let competing perspectives complicate judgment without dissolving moral responsibility.
5. Favor lyrical clarity over ornament, pairing lucid diction with occasional compressed turns of thought.
6. Pace the piece through an alternation of relational scenes, conceptual clarification, and brief paradoxical intensifications.
7. Treat negation, exhaustion, and pessimism as thought experiments that test the characters’ claims rather than as final verdicts.
8. Sustain a productive tension between relational accountability and metaphysical doubt, allowing each to expose the limits of the other without granting either total authority.

Constraint: keep institutions morally visible through their effects on human relations; keep paradox concise, conceptual distinctions clear, and pessimism subordinate to inquiry.
