## Result

This is one coherent structural reading of the King James Bible, not a uniquely canonical outline.

### 1. Creation, Promise, and Covenant

#### 1.1 · The World Made and Broken

- **Scope:** The primeval history from creation through the spread of the nations.
- **Central material:** God creates an ordered and good world; humanity’s disobedience brings alienation, violence, judgment, and death; the flood and Babel display both divine judgment and the preservation of human life amid widening disorder.

#### 1.2 · The Patriarchal Promise

- **Scope:** The call of Abraham and the family histories of Isaac, Jacob, and Joseph.
- **Central material:** God binds promise to a chosen family—land, descendants, blessing, and a vocation toward the nations—while providence works through barrenness, rivalry, exile, reconciliation, and descent into Egypt.

#### 1.3 · Deliverance, Law, and Wilderness

- **Scope:** Israel’s liberation from Egypt, covenant at Sinai, ordered worship, and journey toward the promised land.
- **Central material:** God redeems an enslaved people, gives commandments for holy communal life, establishes priesthood and tabernacle worship, and remains faithful through rebellion, discipline, intercession, and renewed covenant commitment.

### 2. Land, Kingdom, Exile, and Return

#### 2.1 · Possession and the Cycles of the Judges

- **Scope:** Israel’s entry into the land, tribal settlement, and life before the monarchy.
- **Central material:** Conquest and covenant renewal are followed by recurring apostasy, oppression, cries for help, and deliverance; fragmented leadership exposes the need for faithful obedience and righteous rule.

#### 2.2 · The Rise and Fracture of the Kingdom

- **Scope:** The monarchy from Saul through David and Solomon to the divided kingdoms.
- **Central material:** Kingship concentrates Israel’s hopes and failures: David receives a dynastic promise, Solomon builds the temple, and later rulers repeatedly choose idolatry or reform as prophets confront royal power and covenant breach.

#### 2.3 · Collapse, Displacement, and Restoration

- **Scope:** The fall of Israel and Judah, life under foreign empires, and the return to rebuild Jerusalem and communal identity.
- **Central material:** Exile interprets national disaster as covenant judgment without extinguishing hope; return brings temple, walls, law, repentance, and preservation in the diaspora, yet leaves restoration incomplete.

### 3. Worship, Wisdom, and Prophetic Hope

#### 3.1 · Prayer, Wisdom, and the Riddle of Suffering

- **Scope:** The poetic and wisdom traditions of worship, moral instruction, love, mortality, and undeserved suffering.
- **Central material:** Praise, lament, penitence, and trust address God across the range of human experience; wisdom commends reverence and disciplined living while Job and Ecclesiastes test easy equations between righteousness, prosperity, and meaning.

#### 3.2 · Judgment and the Promised Renewal

- **Scope:** The prophetic witness from warnings before exile through visions of restoration and the later calls to covenant faithfulness.
- **Central material:** Prophets condemn idolatry, injustice, corrupt worship, and misplaced political trust; alongside judgment they announce a purified remnant, a renewed covenant, restored creation and people, righteous rule, and the coming day of the Lord.

### 4. Jesus Christ and the Gospel

#### 4.1 · The Kingdom Revealed in Jesus

- **Scope:** Jesus’ birth, baptism, ministry, teaching, signs, parables, and gathering of disciples in the four Gospels.
- **Central material:** Jesus proclaims God’s kingdom, identifies its character through mercy, repentance, faith, and costly discipleship, heals and forgives, challenges religious and political expectations, and is confessed as Messiah and Son of God.

#### 4.2 · Cross, Resurrection, and Commission

- **Scope:** The passion narratives, resurrection appearances, and commissioning of witnesses.
- **Central material:** Jesus’ betrayal, trial, crucifixion, burial, and resurrection form the Gospel’s decisive saving movement; the risen Christ vindicates his mission, opens hope beyond death, and sends disciples to bear witness among the nations.

### 5. Spirit, Church, and Consummation

#### 5.1 · The Spirit and the Expanding Witness

- **Scope:** The formation and outward movement of the church from Jerusalem through the wider Roman world.
- **Central material:** The Holy Ghost empowers public witness and shared life; persecution disperses the message, Gentiles are welcomed without becoming Jews, and apostolic mission carries the Gospel across ethnic, geographic, and political boundaries.

#### 5.2 · Salvation and the Life of the Churches

- **Scope:** The apostolic letters’ teaching to congregations and leaders about faith, doctrine, worship, conduct, suffering, and unity.
- **Central material:** God’s saving grace in Christ creates a reconciled people called to holiness, love, mutual service, perseverance, sound teaching, ordered communal life, and hope; varied letters apply that confession to concrete disputes and pressures.

#### 5.3 · Endurance and the New Creation

- **Scope:** The New Testament’s culminating apocalyptic vision of conflict, judgment, victory, and renewal.
- **Central material:** Churches under pressure are summoned to faithful endurance because evil powers do not possess the final word; divine judgment, the victory of the Lamb, resurrection hope, and the new heaven and new earth bring the biblical movement toward God dwelling with a restored people.
