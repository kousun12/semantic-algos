# 035 · leaf-4-2-forge

- Function: repository `question-forge`

## Result

## The question you asked

If Christ’s priesthood is sufficient, what place should costly obedience, resistance to false teaching, holy love, faithful suffering, and apostolic hope have in a believer’s perseverance?

## What it's doing

The question names a genuine tension: how Christ’s sufficient work relates to the believer’s costly perseverance. But by asking what “place” these things have “in a believer,” it keeps the matter safely at the level of theological arrangement. It also risks smuggling in a binary—either Christ’s priesthood is sufficient or human faithfulness meaningfully matters—when the harder question is how to live without turning grace into permission to retreat or turning obedience into a claim of self-sufficiency. What remains protected is the personal cost of discovering which distortion becomes attractive when faithfulness threatens comfort, belonging, safety, or relief.

## The forged question

**When following Christ costs me comfort, belonging, safety, or relief, how can I entrust my perseverance wholly to his sufficient priesthood while embracing costly obedience, resisting false teaching, practicing holy love, suffering faithfully, and sustaining apostolic hope—and what would expose that I have turned either grace into an excuse for retreat or faithfulness into a claim upon God?**

## What changed

The question shifts the subject from “a believer” to “me,” moves from assigning doctrines their proper place to discerning a lived posture, and dramatizes the stakes through concrete losses. It preserves the weight on both sides: Christ’s priesthood must remain wholly sufficient, and faithfulness must remain genuinely costly. By naming two opposed corruptions—retreat disguised as trust and self-reliance disguised as obedience—the forged version resists an easy doctrinal slogan and asks for honest self-recognition.

## Living with it

Carry the question into moments when truth, love, endurance, or hope asks for something tangible. Notice which cost you most want Christ’s sufficiency to remove, and which act of faithfulness most tempts you to regard yourself as spiritually secure. Let those moments keep the question concrete without forcing a quick resolution.
